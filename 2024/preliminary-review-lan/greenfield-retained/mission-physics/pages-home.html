01Model the game

What can a robot score, and when?

Turn the release-day manual into a model of the game before drawing a robot. Count the pieces, locate them, identify scoring targets and split the match into useful time windows.

Identify the governing manual and its edition before tracing a route from a starting location through a piece source to a scoring zone. Compare that route with the paths available to partners and opponents.

Carry forward

A dated game model with citations, piece inventory, field coordinates, phase lengths and a list of unknowns.

02Conceptual design

What changes when the robot changes shape?

Give students an adjustable box and a scale field. Try different widths, headings and approaches using the same manual edition.

In conceptual design, describe the concept of operations (ConOps) and compare match scenarios with a robot-sized prism. Use field rehearsal to compare headings, approach faces and assumed acquisition, shot and reset times. Play three robots on each alliance. Watch the front intake pursue a marked loose piece. Follow the hopper and live score as each delivered piece clears the feeder at the project’s modeled rate. Compare distinct opening assignments and wait times. Compare one more scoring trip with switching to a terminal objective. Change the switch time and the assumed support at assessment, then compare ordinary points, occupied slots and terminal awards on the same match clock.

Carry forward

Candidate cycles, interaction faces, field poses, assumed times and student observations that the team can cite in its match story.

03Choose a match role

Which scoring cycle should our team own?

Use the field observations to tell two plausible match stories. Compare the contribution each role could make, the time it needs, the work left for partners and a fallback.

Replay a specialist and a complementary role against the same partners and opposition. Compare individual waits, available terminal slots and the alliance margin. Allocate the pieces likely to reach one robot in one window. Subtract time spent traveling and aligning. Compare the required pieces per second with a candidate loader’s repeatable cycle time.

Carry forward

A team-selected role, a repeatable cycle, a fallback and the assumption most likely to change the choice.

04Specify capabilities

What must the robot do for this story to hold?

Translate each important part of the chosen cycle into a requirement with units, operating conditions and a measurable acceptance check.

Specify the capture cadence. If route overlap exposes the intake, identify the contact direction and assumptions about speed, mass and compliance. Define which function must survive and how to check it afterward.

Carry forward

A capability contract tying each requirement to a rule, observation or assumption and a planned verification activity.

Can a twelve-piece hopper deliver seven pieces in six seconds?

Seven pieces can reach the outlet. For each, the gate needs 0.25 s, the transfer 0.50 s and the receiver 0.75 s. Count a handoff when the receiver accepts the piece.

  • One interlock runs the stages in sequence: 4 handoffs.
  • Independent stages overlap: 7 handoffs. The first takes 1.50 s. The receiver then accepts one every 0.75 s.
  • A fixed guide closes the port: 0 handoffs.

Which two consecutive pieces would you track to test the overlap? Record their port crossings, receiver signals and reset times.

05Preliminary and detailed design

Which design can meet the contract?

Export the focal robot’s finite scoring window from the field lab. Compare how different whole-robot architectures serve that window before allocating subsystems. Compare their resource use on the same nominal and adverse match cases. Keep geometry, sensor frames, actuator behavior and mode transitions together as the design changes.

Read the complete strategic narrative before placing components. Record each objective and fallback, including endgame. Select each solid in the review to inspect its purpose and the reason it earns space in the layout. Place the major components in a top-down assembly layout. Label the representative solids and inspect the animation and numerical clearance on the same trajectory. Check the complete weight range and weak-pack voltage before detailed CAD. In detailed design, use actual purchased parts where available and animate the installed Fusion joints. Start with four-module swerve unless another drivetrain is specified. Compare supplier and shop-built options. Trace the optical path through the seated piece. Move it toward the exit and check whether the last port clears while the piece still rests on the mechanism.

Carry forward

A versioned CAD and control candidate, a named fallback, and testable subsystem interfaces.

Could the design premise be wrong?

A camera sees a part sitting on a moving tray. Does that image prove the tray can hold it through a turn?

Checking position gives permit. Include a loose part with the same outline and the same image gives hold. Both seated-and-secured and seated-but-loose fit.

Compare a better camera view with sensing grip or changing how the tray captures the part. Test which one distinguishes the failure.

06Test and revise

Which decision does the evidence support?

Compare the installed scoring rate with the chosen role over its exact clock window. Freeze the trial settings and include a held-out case when refining the design. Test whether two supports can carry the weight while the robot still rotates about the line joining them. Trace every path through acceleration, hold and power loss. Record the exact candidate, inputs, observed result and limitations.

Locate each installed mount against its drawing datums. If two CAD measurements disagree about whether solids share material, probe both at the same installed coordinates before deciding which part needs correction. When the STEP intersection has invalid geometry, open the two named native bodies and inspect the contact at the same pose. If identical installed fits give different volumes at different robot corners, inspect the named pair at each corner and compare the native measurements. Follow a piece from its donor onto the commissioned receiving support. Measure the load the receiver carries and the time the whole piece remains inside its intended region. Check the departing support for remaining contact. Check where every robot part travels as the mechanism extends, including at the scoring pose. A sensor may see the piece enter the goal while a slide strikes the field. Trace installed loads through structural hardware into the chassis. Test a compression-only seat against simultaneous uplift, side load and tipping before counting on a bracket to hold it down. A nearby cover can hide a gap underneath a bracket. Can the bolted parts pull through the frame together? Move the complete stack in the load direction, identify its stop and check the load that stop can carry. Compare the recorded path with the full robot and field inventories. Replay the same loaded trip against the other five robots on the match clock; locate the first occupied lane and trace which later handoffs are still reachable. Then shift the target map toward the tightest clearance. Compare the observer decision with the margin in an independent field frame.

Carry forward

Review the detailed design and fabrication package on the exact saved Fusion issue. A build-ready outcome requires passing the applicable installed checks. Compare every installed body, test the service and operating paths, and show the governing load and repeated trials. Promote the model after its mechanical review passes.
